Concept study of a generation interstellar spaceship | January-May 2025

Concept study developed for the Project Hyperion design competition by the Initiative for Interstellar Studies. My team won the 1st prize of the competition exploring the feasibility of crewed interstellar travel via generation spaceships, using current and near-future technologies. The idea behind a generation ship is that the initial crew would live, reproduce, and die on the ship, with their descendants continuing the journey until reaching the destination. These starships are often envisioned as self-sustaining ecosystems, featuring agriculture, habitation, and other necessary life-support systems to ensure survival across multiple generations. In our research and design exploration our multidisciplinary team aimed to include and integrate in the process all relevant areas of knowledge that could possibly drive the creation of an interstellar starship ark. Engineering, physics, biology, artificial intelligence, cybernetic, urban architecture, psychology, anthropo-sociological and teleological disciplines are among the disciplines that we encompassed in our research.

Space Economy

Orbital coexistence: preventing LEO ecosystem failure through multilateral special orbital economic zone | May-June 2026

The projected $1.8 trillion space economy by 2035 comes with an ever-increasing risk of collapse of the Earth-orbiting ecosystem. The primary causes of a future Kessler syndrome scenario that will render LEO orbits unusable for decades are multiple and interconnected: the impending proliferation of mega-constellations, the incompatible use of orbital slots by competing nations, the increasingly hybrid (civilian-military) use of space assets and infrastructure and their central role in geopolitical dynamics, as well as the lack of a unified orbital traffic management system.

This proposal aims to establish a new model of shared governance in Earth’s orbits as a prerequisite for the long-term sustainable development of human civilization on Earth and the peaceful expansion of humanity to the Moon, Mars, and beyond. The ITU World Radiocommunication Conference 2027 (WRC-27), which will take place in Shanghai this October, will be pivotal in shaping the future of international relations and diplomacy regarding Earth’s orbital space. The major spacefaring nations must decide whether to embark on a new era of cooperation and coexistence in outer space or continue on a path of uncontrolled, competitive growth, which is destined to cause the collapse of the orbital ecosystem.

The proposed strategy involves establishing a new model for international governance and a co-financed international commercial initiative aimed at creating the first International Solar-powered Orbital Grid that will use massive, space-based solar arrays to harvest uninterrupted sunlight, converting it into laser/microwaves to beam down to Earth's power grids or to in-orbit infrastructure. Through a limited multilateral executive agreement, spacefaring member states will establish an International Space Agency with special jurisdiction over specific economic zones in Medium Earth Orbit (MEO) orbital slots. These zones will enable the creation of the ISOG satellite infrastructure, which aims to promote economic interdependence among nations with space programs and distribute the benefits of space resources to various private actors and nations worldwide.

Physics Lab

Design and construction of a microdosimetric detector for the International Space Station (ISS) | September 2010 - April 2011

MSc Physics thesis project work @ INFN (Istituto Nazionale di Fisica Nucleare) focused on the design and construction of a microdosimetric detector for the International Space Station (ISS) in order to simulate human tissues to obtain data of the damages provoked by ionizing radiation inside the habitable modules. I was involved in the scientific design, configuration of the device and the technical and electronic testing of the detector’s components in the INFN laboratories. Microdosimetric detectors can accurately measure the spectrum and intensity of different types of radiation, providing information on the resulting biological effects.

Astronauts in the International Space Station experiment high doses of radiation during their 6-months missions. This high radio pollution from the Sun's radiation could cause severe illness and sickness at organs and tissues of the human body. The design of appropriate detectors to simulate human-body tissues (like skin or muscles) can be achieved with appropriate materials that have the same pattern of interaction with the radiation as the human body. Microdosimetric detectors can then measure spectrum and intensity of different types of radiation giving informations on the resulting biological effects. Different types of ionizing radiations are being studied in this thesis accordingly to the same radiation pattern encountered aboard the ISS. Designing and configuration of the electronic of the device has been conducted in the laboratory together with the configuration of the instrument. Exposure of the detector to external radioactive sources has been performed in order to measure the doses of different types of exposures. In this way has been possible to create a detector capable to evaluate doses of radiation experimented in the same way of that of human tissues.
